# MH-0 generation-11 production radial-kernel profile

Date: 2026-09-11

## Result

Nsight Systems and Nsight Compute were run on the generation-11 production
`streamed_edges="direct"` capacity path. The input is a rattled 4,000-atom AlN
cell, and geometry, radial metadata, and harmonics are regenerated by the normal
production path before the captured evaluation.

The retained R1-reverse source-feature cache works through the intended
mechanism. Relative to generation 10, it removes 5.760 million dynamic FP32
global-load requests and 23.040 million theoretical L2 sectors from R1 reverse.
Nsight Compute replay time falls by 2.51%, and the independent Systems timeline
shows a 2.91% kernel reduction. Since R1 reverse accounts for 22.1% of GPU
kernel time, the measured full-evaluator improvement is 0.56% at 4,000 atoms.

The remaining kernel limits are:

- R1 forward: 79.87% long-scoreboard stalls, 82.73% L2 throughput, and only
  5.37% DRAM throughput.
- R1 reverse: 52.19% long-scoreboard stalls after caching, 86.06% L2
  throughput, and 4.56% DRAM throughput.
- R0 forward: 64.52% long-scoreboard and 18.56% short-scoreboard stalls, with
  63.62% SM throughput.
- R0 reverse: a mixed latency, arithmetic, and launch-wave case. L2 throughput
  is only 18.60%, so an L2-only change is unlikely to move it substantially.

These results support the shared source cache already retained in generation
11. They do not support the rejected two-source warp-atomic schedule, more
scalar-to-vector coefficient repacking, or treating any of these kernels as
DRAM-bandwidth bound.

Source-correlated samples place the largest long-scoreboard waits on
conversions, FMAs, and multiplies that consume recently loaded radii, spline
coefficients, features, harmonics, or adjoints. The leading not-issued
long-scoreboard samples are 24,970 for R1 forward, 8,925 for cached R1 reverse,
18,320 for R0 forward, and 15,314 for R0 reverse. This remains a dependent-load
latency signature rather than a load-instruction issue-throttle signature.

R1 reverse's lower branch efficiency comes partly from the lane-specialized
harmonic-gradient evaluator. Branch resolution accounts for 5.18% of active
warp stalls, well below long scoreboards, so removing the switch is secondary.

## Source-cache A/B

The accepted cache cooperatively loads the source's `4 x 128` FP32 feature slab
once per source and reuses it for all outgoing edges. The table compares the
generation-10 and generation-11 R1 fused reverse kernels.

| Metric | Gen 10 baseline | Gen 11 source cache | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NCU replay duration | 2.467488 ms | 2.405504 ms | **-2.51%** |
| 32-bit global load requests | 143.496 M | 137.736 M | **-4.01%** |
| 32-bit global load L2 sectors | 510.600 M | 487.560 M | **-4.51%** |
| All theoretical global L2 sectors | 515.224 M | 492.184 M | **-4.47%** |
| Shared 32-bit load instructions | 6.980 M | 12.804 M | +5.824 M |
| L2 throughput | 87.09% | 86.06% | -1.03 points |
| Long-scoreboard stall | 54.81% | 52.19% | -2.62 points |
| SM throughput | 45.75% | 47.01% | +1.26 points |
| Registers/thread | 96 | 96 | unchanged |
| Achieved occupancy | 32.04% | 31.96% | -0.08 points |
| Shared allocation/block | 3,104 B | 5,152 B | +2,048 B |
| Local/shared spills | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | unchanged |

The static kernel grows from 3,000 to 3,072 SASS instructions. Static `LDG`
count increases from 208 to 211, while `LDS` grows from 14 to 18 and `STS` from
18 to 25. That does not contradict the dynamic-load reduction: the cache adds
a small cooperative global-load prologue, while replacing loads executed on
every edge with shared loads in the hot loop.

The cache does not improve occupancy, and it removes only 4.5% of the kernel's
global sectors. Its 2.5-2.9% kernel gain is therefore consistent with the
measured traffic change. Applying the 22.1% kernel share predicts roughly a
0.6% evaluator gain, matching the measured 0.56%.

## Global access shape

Source counters report ideal coalescing for R1 and R0-forward loads. R0 reverse
has 1.624 million excessive sectors across loads and stores, 0.49% of its
331.814 million requested sectors, so coalescing is not its primary limit.

| Kernel | 32-bit load requests | 32-bit L2 sectors | 64-bit load requests | 64-bit L2 sectors |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| R1 forward | 93.248 M | 285.440 M | 2.912 M | 2.912 M |
| R1 reverse, cached | 137.736 M | 487.560 M | 3.276 M | 3.276 M |
| R0 forward | 35.008 M | 104.896 M | 2.928 M | 2.928 M |
| R0 reverse | 234.300 M | 327.278 M | 2.415 M | 2.716 M |

For R1 forward, every edge/channel warp executes 64 dynamic 32-bit load
instructions. Forty are scalar spline-coefficient loads. Each coefficient load
is coalesced into four 32-byte sectors, so coefficients account for 160 of
approximately 196 L2 sectors per edge/channel warp. Repacking those forty
scalars as ten `float4` loads leaves the same 160 sectors and therefore did not
improve the production kernel despite reducing static load instructions.

## Optimization implications

1. **Keep the generation-11 R1-reverse source cache.** It removes real dynamic
   traffic with exact production parity and a robust 0.56% full-evaluator gain.
   The cache result is now measured rather than proposed.

2. **Reduce R1-forward coefficient sectors.** Its 79.87% long-scoreboard share
   and 82.73% L2 throughput remain the clearest opportunity. A focused next
   experiment is normalized-interval cubic coefficients stored in FP16 and
   expanded to FP32 in registers. This halves coefficient sectors; it must pass
   radial value/derivative error checks and full energy, force, and stress
   parity before production timing.

3. **Tile R1-forward output accumulators only if it crosses an occupancy
   threshold.** The 96-register allocation limits the kernel to two blocks per
   SM. Compile-time 20- or 10-output bands are useful only if they reach at most
   80 registers without duplicating enough edge and coefficient traffic to
   erase the latency-hiding gain.

4. **Treat R0 reverse as a mixed problem.** Its 18.60% L2 throughput does not
   justify coefficient caching as the first experiment. Source-correlated
   stalls instead support reducing the dependent multiply/FMA chain, while a
   510-block persistent launch is a separate, narrow test for removing the
   partial second wave.

5. **Do not revive the two-source warp-atomic schedule in its tested form.** It
   was 2.76% slower than cache-only at 4,000 atoms. Any future multi-owner
   design must preserve independent 64-thread reductions and avoid the added
   FP64 edge-force atomics before it merits another production trial.
